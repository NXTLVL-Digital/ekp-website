# EKP Website Copy Plan — The Heirloom Positioning

**Recovered and consolidated 2026-07-31** from the May 2026 research package
(`deliverables/2026-05-11-ekp-premium-package/research/` + `deliverables/2026-05-04-ekp-design-options/research/`
+ the three concept rationales). This file lives in the repo so the plan can never

## 1. Positioning statement (the north star)

> For high school seniors and families in the Danville–Lynchburg–Smith Mountain
> Lake corridor who want their portraits to feel like the cover of a magazine
> instead of a yearbook page, Emily Kathryn Photography creates a guided,
> gender-inclusive editorial portrait experience **that ends in wall art and
> albums they'll keep for a generation.**

The one-line version, and the headline voice of the whole site:

> **"Portraits made for the wall, not the phone."**

Why this wins: the market research found the local market is overwhelmingly
digital-files-first. "Almost no one in the Danville-to-Lynchburg corridor is
selling a boutique editorial experience with heirloom-grade physical product,
in-person sales, and a confident parent-trust narrative. That is the air gap
EKP can move into." Every aspirational benchmark studio leads with heirloom
products; no local competitor does. This is EKP's differentiator, and the copy's
job is to make it felt on every page.

**Critical nuance:** digital files are still non-negotiable for the primary
buyer — "she will not book a photographer who doesn't include these in some
way." Products are positioned **on top of** files, never instead of them.

## 2. Who the copy is written for

Two readers at once. Rule: "if a line only resonates with one, rewrite it."

**Rachel — the Memory-Keeper Mom (primary buyer, the product customer).**
42–55, Forest/Danville/Chatham corridor, HH income $95K–$140K. Treats senior
year as "the last major milestone of her child's childhood… with the emotional
weight of a wedding, scaled down." Reads the About page. Will not blink at
$1,500–$2,500 for the right experience; will choke at $1,500 for a generic one.
Buys, in order: digital files → one signature wall piece ($400–$1,200) → a
senior album ($700–$1,500, "the heirloom anchor of the purchase") → gift prints
for grandparents. Typical guided investment: $1,800–$3,500. More willing to
upgrade at the ordering appointment than to commit big at booking.

**Ella — the Style-Aware Senior (the influencer).** 17, TikTok-primary,
4-second vibe check. Wants "a Vogue shoot, not a yearbook." Doesn't care about
wall art — "that's her mother's domain" — but will brag about a senior album if
it's framed as a milestone marker. Copy speaks to her through confidence and
editorial styling, never by trying to sound young.

**Maren — the Lake-House Mom (highest product spend, growth vertical).**
48–62, Smith Mountain Lake second home, HH $250K+. "Time is scarcer than
money." Wants framed portraits that belong in a designed space; values physical
over digital. Buys large-format wall art ($800–$2,500+), heirloom family album
($1,000–$2,000). Session totals $2,500–$6,000+. Her register is legacy: "a
family photo on the dock before everyone gets too old for this."

## 3. Voice rules (from the brand guide — binding)

Six attributes: **Warm** (trusted friend, not bubbly) · **Editorial** (composed
rhythm, not cold) · **Confident** (doesn't oversell) · **Specific** (names the
place, the school, the moment) · **Parent-Clear** (readable in 30 seconds) ·
**Senior-Aware** (no talking down, no trying to sound young).

Seven rules:
1. Lead with emotion, support with proof.
2. Write to senior and parent together.
3. Earn the premium price in every paragraph — "stop writing every time you've
   written something that could appear on any photographer's website."
4. No empty claims — if you say "magazine-worthy," a photograph nearby must earn it.
5. No participation-trophy adjectives (*amazing, incredible, fabulous,
   gorgeous, beautiful* alone) unless paired with a specific reason.
6. Direct FAQ answers, no hedging.
7. **Never "capture"** — use *create, make, photograph, bring out, direct*.

**Words to use:** editorial · designed · guided · personal · confident ·
crafted · considered · heirloom · magazine · studio · portrait · frame ·
composed · permanent · made.

**Words to avoid:** capture · memories · amazing · incredible · fabulous ·
stunning · best · top · quick · easy · affordable · cheap · snap · pose.

CTAs are inquiry-anchored, not "book now." Reference lines:
- Hero: "Senior portraits that hold up on the wall. / Editorial portraiture, made in South-Central Virginia."
- Service opener: "One subject. One afternoon. A magazine you'll still want to open in twenty years."
- Closing CTA: "Let's plan something worth keeping."
- Investment anchor: "Sessions begin at $799. Most families invest between $1,800 and $3,500 across digital files, wall art, and a senior album."

## 3b. Rhythm and humanity (binding — added 2026-07-31 after Jeff's review)

These outrank everything below when they conflict:

1. **Zero em dashes (—) and en dashes (–) in copy.** They read as AI writing. Restructure into short sentences, commas, or periods.
2. **Human and natural above all.** Contractions on. Everyday vocabulary. No words or phrases people don't use in normal conversation. No defensive sales phrases ("no pressure, no obligation").
3. **State the theme once per page, twice at most.** The wall-not-phone position is a thesis, not a chorus. Repetition reads as a sales script; an editorial voice says it once and lets the photographs argue.
4. **The hooks bank below is research language for inspiration, NOT slogans to repeat verbatim.** One near-verbatim use per hook across the whole site is plenty.
5. **Short declarative rhythm** per the guide's samples: "One subject. One afternoon. A magazine you'll still want to open in twenty years." Vary sentence length; avoid long compound sentences.
6. **Proof over adjectives.** Tenure ("more than a decade"), real places, real process beats. The site should never assert "editorial" more than twice per page.
7. **Vary CTAs across pages.** Never four variants of the same closer.
8. **Boys-inclusive pronouns** on About/process copy; don't default seniors to "she".
9. **Specificity bank needed from Emily:** school names, favorite landmarks, identity props (truck, jersey, instrument, FFA jacket, family farm). Until she supplies it, use only real geography already on the site; never invent specifics.

## 4. Emotional hooks bank (voice-of-customer — inspiration, not slogans; see 3b.4)

- "I want photos I can hang up — not just files on my phone." (Rachel — the core thread)
- "This is the last big chapter of her childhood. I want to do this right."
- "I want her to actually feel beautiful — not just look beautiful in pictures."
- "She's only a senior once." / "We don't do this every day."
- "I want her to look like herself, just elevated."
- "I want it to look like a Vogue shoot, not a yearbook." (Ella)
- "These look like fake yearbook photos." (Ella's fear — never let the work or words evoke this)
- "I want a photo we can frame for above the mantel." (Maren)
- "We're never all together — when we are, I want one for each of the kids to take home." (Maren)
- Objection to defuse with tiered collections, never discounts: "I don't want to spend $3,000 on photos."

## 5. Message architecture per page

Every page keeps one distinct job (SEO rule: no duplicated blocks across pages).

| Page | Job | Product thread requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Home | Make the differentiator felt in 5 seconds | Hero states the wall-not-phone position; portfolio spreads framed as "made for walls" |
| Senior Portraits | Experience, transformation, timeline, **products**, FAQ | Album as the milestone artifact; senior-boys section; FAQ: "Do parents receive help choosing prints and albums?" |
| Family Portraits | Same structure for families + SML vertical | Wall art / mantel language (Maren's register) |
| Investment | "Starting price, what is included, and what comes next" | The ordering appointment explained; collections not à la carte; heirloom product gallery; the $1,800–$3,500 anchor sentence |
| About | Rachel's trust read | Why Emily believes portraits belong on walls (personal conviction, not sales) |
| Raves | Proof | Testimonials that mention products/albums get priority placement |
| Style Guide | Ella's surface, parent-clear | Ties styling to how portraits print and frame |
| Contact | Zero-friction inquiry | Reassure process: session → ordering appointment → heirloom delivery |
| City pages | Local relevance, unique per city | AEO answer includes the product differentiator ("boutique experience ending in printed artwork" vs "digital-only photographer") |
| Journal | Cornerstone content | First cornerstone post: "Why Wall Art Matters More Than Files" |

**AEO constraints:** answer blocks are plain-language Q&A, 2–4 sentences then
detail, FAQ schema, one H1 per page, conversion copy in server-rendered HTML
(already true). Two product-critical questions to answer somewhere on the site:
"Do parents receive help choosing prints and albums?" and "What is the
difference between a digital-only photographer and a boutique senior portrait
experience?"

## 6. Truth requirements (claims must be earned before they ship)

- Senior-boys positioning needs 18+ real senior-boy portraits published.
- SML/lake vertical claims need 12+ real SML family portraits.
- "Heirloom products" claims need a real product menu / ordering process Emily
  actually offers (IPS tooling like Fundy or Swift Galleries — business
  decision, not website work).
- Any "magazine" language needs the editorial portfolio nearby to earn it.

## 7. Current-site gap audit (2026-07-31)

The engineering is done; the copy has not yet caught up to this plan:

1. **The differentiator is nearly silent.** Album/heirloom/wall-art language
   appears in only 2 of 10 core pages (13 mentions total — investment and
   senior-portraits). Homepage, family, about, raves, style-guide: zero. The
   single thing that sets EKP apart is not being said.
2. **20+ banned-word violations** — "capture/memories/amazing/stunning" family
   across family-portraits (~7), senior-portraits (~5), style-guide (~3),
   investment (2), homepage, raves, city ServiceCards.
3. **No ordering-appointment story.** The session → ordering appointment →
   heirloom delivery arc (the IPS revenue lever: 2x–3x revenue per client)
   appears nowhere.
4. **Maren doesn't exist yet.** No SML/lake-house register on the family page;
   the smith-mountain-lake city page carries the only lake copy.
5. **Investment page anchor missing.** The "most families invest between
   $1,800 and $3,500" sentence — the plan's price-conditioning move — is absent
   (page shows placeholder $400 CMS fallback pricing until Sanity is populated).
6. **FAQ product questions absent.** Neither product-critical AEO question is
   answered on any page.
