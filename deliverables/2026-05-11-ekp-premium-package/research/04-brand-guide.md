# Brand Guide

A working brand guide for Emily Kathryn Photography — built to honor the existing identity, tighten what works, expand what's missing, and align every visible decision to the avatars and competitive position from earlier in this package.

---

## How to Use This Guide

This brand guide is for anyone touching the EKP visual or verbal identity — website, social, packaging, print, email. It is paired with the existing Official Brand Board (logo system, palette, typefaces) and supersedes any ad-hoc decisions made elsewhere. When in doubt, defer to the principles in this document over inherited assumptions.

Every choice in this guide is tied back to a *reason* — the avatar it serves, the competitive position it reinforces, or the market behavior it addresses. The principles matter more than the rules.

---

## 1. Brand Essence

**Editorial portraiture for the people of South-Central Virginia — designed so the senior or family on the other side of the camera walks away feeling like the most confident, recognizable version of themselves.**

EKP is not a wedding photographer. Not a school photographer. Not a corporate headshot photographer. EKP is a *portrait studio with a magazine sensibility*, anchored in a specific geography, deliberately limited to two service lines (senior + family) so each one can be done exceptionally.

The work is editorial without being cold. Personal without being precious. Polished without being posed. Premium without being inaccessible. **A magazine that knows your name.**

---

## 2. Brand Position

### Positioning Statement

> For high school seniors and families in the Danville–Lynchburg–Smith Mountain Lake corridor who want their portraits to feel like the cover of a magazine instead of a yearbook page, Emily Kathryn Photography creates a guided, gender-inclusive editorial portrait experience that ends in wall art and albums they'll keep for a generation.

### What This Means in Practice

- EKP is **not** a value player. EKP is at the upper-boutique tier and grows toward heirloom.
- EKP is **not** the warm-and-soft family photographer. That lane is well-occupied locally. EKP is the *editorial, identity-led* alternative.
- EKP is **not** a one-vertical studio. Senior + family are the two flagship service lines. Branding portraits and SML lifestyle family work are growth lines.
- EKP **is** the only studio in the corridor that markets to senior boys with the same polish as senior girls.
- EKP **is** the studio whose ordering appointment matters as much as the session — because that's where wall art and albums are sold.

### The Two-Buyer Equation

The brand must work simultaneously for:

- **The buyer (Rachel, 47).** Needs to trust the experience, see the value, feel that her daughter is in good hands, and have a clear next step.
- **The influencer (Ella, 17).** Needs the work to look like fashion editorial, the experience to feel like *content*, and the photographer to feel cool.

Every page, post, and email must pass both filters.

## 4. Logo System

The Official Brand Board's logo system stays intact and is the canonical reference.

### Primary Logo — `emily kathryn` wordmark

Set in a tall, condensed editorial serif (the *Yo Andy* feel) with **PHOTOGRAPHY** in spaced caps Acrom underneath. This is the primary signature on the website header, marketing materials, and most public-facing surfaces.

**Use when:** the brand is being introduced for the first time, on covers, on the website header, in social bio art, on print collateral.

**Do not:**
- Stretch or compress the wordmark
- Place over busy photography without a translucent overlay
- Use the wordmark smaller than 90px wide on screen / 1.25in on print

### Alternate Logo — Stacked `emily / kathryn`

Stacked wordmark for tall, narrow applications — Instagram post bottoms, square-ratio marketing collateral, sidebars.

### Submark — `ek.` Circle Monogram

Circle frame with **ek.** at center and *Emily Kathryn Photography* arc'd around the inside. Used on social profile icons, watermarks, packaging seals, and as a small accent in layouts.

### Logo Clear Space and Sizing

Minimum clear space around any logo lockup = the height of the wordmark's lowercase x-height on all sides. Minimum web size for the primary logo is 90px wide. Minimum print size is 1.25 inches wide.

### Web-Safe Type Substitutes (Production)

Because *Yo Andy* and *Acrom* are not freely web-licensed for production deployment, the digital implementation should use the closest licensable equivalents:

| Brand Board Reference | Web-Safe Substitute (Google Fonts) | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Yo Andy* (tall editorial serif) | **Italiana** or **Cormorant Garamond** | Display headlines, large titles |
| *Yo Andy* alt | **DM Serif Display** | Editorial subheads |
| *Acrom* (geometric sans) | **Inter** or **Manrope** | Body, navigation, UI labels |

The Brand Board fonts should still be licensed and used for printed collateral and signature pieces.

## 5. Color System

### Brand Palette (Locked — Brand Board)

| Name | Hex | RGB | CMYK | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Black** | `#000000` | 0, 0, 0 | 0, 0, 0, 100 | Type, anchors, deep backgrounds |
| **Warm Gold** | `#C2A36C` | 194, 163, 108 | 0, 16, 44, 24 | Premium accent, rules, hairlines, buttons |
| **Blush** | `#DCB6AD` | 220, 182, 173 | 0, 17, 21, 14 | Feminine warmth, soft backgrounds |
| **Aqua / Mint** | `#B3D4CD` | 179, 212, 205 | 16, 0, 3, 17 | Freshness, youth, secondary accent |
| **Soft Gray** | `#D6D4D4` | 214, 212, 212 | 0, 1, 1, 16 | Neutral balance, dividers |

### Extended Working Palette (New, Approved)

Three additional values that complete the system for body type, paper, and depth — derived from the brand palette but expand it for digital legibility and print warmth.

| Name | Hex | RGB | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ink** | `#1A1A1A` | 26, 26, 26 | Body type. Softens 100% black for readability. |
| **Paper / Cream** | `#FAF7F2` | 250, 247, 242 | Page background — warmer than pure white, photo-friendly. |
| **Muted** | `#6B655C` | 107, 101, 92 | Secondary type, captions, metadata. |
| **Soft Rule** | `#E5DFD3` | 229, 223, 211 | Table dividers, soft section separators. |

### Color Direction

- **Anchor in black + cream.** Black is for type and contrast; cream is the paper. Together they create the editorial base.
- **Gold is the only premium signal.** Don't introduce other metallic-feel accents. Gold rules, gold buttons, gold underlines, gold seals — that is the whole premium language.
- **Blush and Aqua are accent-only.** Never let either dominate a page. Used for tints, subtle backgrounds, and small accents.
- **Avoid all-beige.** All-beige reads like a wedding-photographer template. Black-and-cream with gold is what differentiates EKP from the soft-beige local field.
- **Avoid full-page color blocks of blush or aqua.** They flatten the image work.

### Allowable Combinations

| Background | Type | Accent | Used For |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cream `#FAF7F2` | Ink `#1A1A1A` | Gold `#C2A36C` | Default body |
| Black `#000000` | Cream `#FAF7F2` | Gold `#C2A36C` | Hero, section dividers |
| Blush `#DCB6AD` | Ink `#1A1A1A` | Gold `#C2A36C` | Small soft callouts |
| Cream `#FAF7F2` | Ink `#1A1A1A` | Aqua `#B3D4CD` | Senior-Y2K accents only |
| Soft Gray `#D6D4D4` | Ink `#1A1A1A` | — | Quiet utility / forms |

### What to Never Do With Color

- Don't use gold for body text — it reads as low-contrast and tired.
- Don't combine blush and aqua at high saturation in the same view.
- Don't introduce purple, navy, terracotta, sage, or other non-palette colors. The palette is the palette.
- Don't put colored text on busy photo backgrounds. Use the dark/cream overlay first.

## 6. Typography System

### Type Hierarchy

| Level | Typeface | Weight | Size (web) | Size (print) | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Display (D1) | **Italiana** | Regular | 72–120px | 56–96pt | Hero titles, magazine-style headlines |
| Display (D2) | **Cormorant Garamond** | Regular / Italic | 48–72px | 36–56pt | Page titles, section openers |
| Heading (H1) | **Cormorant Garamond** | Regular | 36–48px | 28–36pt | Article headings, large UI |
| Heading (H2) | **Cormorant Garamond** | Semibold | 24–32px | 18–24pt | Subsections |
| Eyebrow | **Inter** | 500 weight, all-caps, 0.3em letter-spacing | 11–13px | 9–11pt | Section labels, eyebrows |
| Body | **Inter** | Regular | 16–18px | 10–11pt | All body type |
| Caption | **Inter** | Regular, italic optional | 12–14px | 8–9pt | Captions, metadata |
| Quote | **Cormorant Garamond Italic** | Regular | 20–32px | 14–18pt | Pull quotes |

### Pairing Rules

- **One serif. One sans.** Don't introduce a third typeface.
- **Italiana for display, Cormorant for everything else serif.** Italiana is too narrow for body type but unmatched at large size.
- **Inter for all sans.** No exceptions in production.
- **Italics for emotion, not for emphasis.** Use italics in serif for quotes, captions, and emotional intent. Use semibold (not italics) in sans for emphasis.

### Letter Spacing

- All-caps eyebrows: **0.28–0.42em** letter-spacing. Generous space is the editorial signature.
- Body type: default browser tracking, no manipulation.
- Display serif: slight negative tracking (-0.005 to -0.02em) at very large sizes to tighten editorial composition.

### Numeric Treatment

Numerals — especially in the "01" / "02" / "03" service or section markers — should be set in **Italiana or Cormorant Garamond Regular**, large, gold-accented, and treated as a design element. Lift this directly from magazine layout convention.

## 7. Photography Direction

The portraits *are* the brand. Type and color frame them; the work has to carry the brand on its own.

### Aesthetic Principles

- **Bright but not washed out.** Highlights retained, shadows present.
- **Color-graded consistently.** A single editing voice across an entire gallery — slight warmth, lifted blacks, restrained saturation.
- **Editorial direction, not lifestyle drift.** Subjects are styled, posed with intent, framed deliberately.
- **Confidence over cuteness.** Genuine expression, but the goal is a posture and gaze that reads composed, not silly.
- **Personality-led.** Locations and props tied to the senior's identity (truck, instrument, jersey, FFA jacket, downtown, family farm).
- **Location-rich.** Show range: fields, downtown brick, water, florals, schools, farm, family interiors.

### Boys-Inclusive Casting

The portfolio currently skews girl-coded. To re-balance:

- Target 30–40% boys in the home page portfolio grid by end of summer 2026
- Senior portfolio page must include a Boys section with at least 18–24 portraits
- Behind-the-scenes social content should feature both with equal frequency
- About page and Process page should reference "seniors" and "the senior" without defaulting to female pronouns

### Don'ts

- No over-dark hero crops that hide the subject
- No generic stock-style detail shots (rings, bouquets, isolated outfit props alone)
- No gallery with no organizing principle — galleries must be either chronological, stylistic, or seasonal
- No tiny thumbnails — the work deserves scale on the page
- No filters that age the work (heavy vintage VSCO, soft-glow dreaminess, faded matte presets)

### Approved Editing Direction

- Warm-leaning whites, true blacks
- Skin tones natural, no over-smoothing
- Slight warmth in midtones, restrained saturation
- Blacks slightly lifted (matte feel) but never crushed flat
- Color consistency across an entire delivery — *not* a mood-mix

## 8. Voice & Tone

### Voice Attributes (Six)

| Attribute | Means | Doesn't Mean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Warm** | Speaks like a trusted friend | Bubbly or overfamiliar |
| **Editorial** | Considered, composed sentence rhythm | Cold or distant |
| **Confident** | Says what's true, doesn't oversell | Boastful or salesy |
| **Specific** | Names the place, the school, the moment | Generic "memories" language |
| **Parent-Clear** | A buyer with limited time can read it in 30 seconds | Insider jargon |
| **Senior-Aware** | The 17-year-old doesn't feel talked down to | Trying too hard to sound young |

### Voice Rules

1. **Lead with emotion, then support with proof.** Don't bury the feeling under specs.
2. **Write to the senior and parent together.** If a line only resonates with one, rewrite it.
3. **Earn the premium price in every paragraph.** Stop writing every time you've written something that could appear on any photographer's website.
4. **No empty claims.** If you say "magazine-worthy," there has to be a photograph nearby that earns the phrase.
5. **No participation-trophy adjectives.** Banish *amazing, beautiful (alone), incredible, fabulous, gorgeous.* Use them only when paired with a specific reason.
6. **Use direct answers in FAQ.** No hedging. The buyer is anxious.
7. **Avoid the word "capture."** Overused in photography. Use *create*, *make*, *photograph*, *bring out*, *direct*.

### Sample Copy

**Hero — Concept A voice (Editorial Heirloom):**
> Senior portraits that hold up on the wall.
> Editorial portraiture, made in South-Central Virginia.

**Hero — Concept B voice (Modern Muse):**
> Senior year is the cover. Let's shoot it like one.

**Hero — Concept C voice (Boutique Atelier):**
> A portrait studio for seniors and families,
> serving the Danville–Lynchburg corridor.

**Service section opener:**
> One subject. One afternoon. A magazine you'll still want to open in twenty years.

**Investment section opener:**
> Sessions begin at $799. Most families invest between $1,800 and $3,500 across digital files, wall art, and a senior album.

**About page opener:**
> Emily Kathryn has photographed seniors and families across South-Central Virginia for over a decade — long enough to know that the right photo doesn't happen by accident.

**FAQ — "How much does this cost?"**
> The session begins at $799. After your session, we'll meet to design wall art, prints, and an album from your favorite images — most families invest between $1,800 and $3,500 in total. We'll send a full investment guide before your session.

**Closing CTA:**
> Let's plan something worth keeping.
> Inquire for senior + family availability.

### Words to Use

*Editorial. Designed. Guided. Personal. Confident. Crafted. Considered. Heirloom. Magazine. Studio. Portrait. Frame. Image. Composed. Permanent. Made.*

### Words to Avoid

*Capture (overused). Memories (generic). Amazing. Incredible. Fabulous. Stunning (overused). Best (vague). Top (vague). Quick. Easy. Affordable (off-brand). Cheap. Snap. Click. Pose.*

### Tone Across Channels

| Channel | Tone | Length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Website | Editorial-confident, parent-clear | Tight paragraphs, no walls of text |
| Instagram caption | Conversational-editorial | 2–4 sentences |
| TikTok caption | Senior-aware, lightly playful | 1–2 sentences |
| Email newsletter | Confident-personal | 200–400 words |
| Inquiry response | Warm-professional | Personal, addressed by name |
| Investment guide | Editorial-luxurious | Full sentences, no bullets where possible |

## 9. Layout Principles

### Whitespace

Whitespace is the most important brand asset after photography. Default toward more than feels comfortable. Editorial layout breathes.

### Grid

- Web: 12-column grid with 24px gutter on desktop, 8px on mobile
- Print: 6-column grid, 0.25-inch gutter
- Photography breaks the grid. Type respects the grid.

### Rhythm

A page should alternate: hero photograph → tight type block → image → image-pair → quote → image → CTA. The visual cadence is *photograph, type, breath, photograph*.

### Page Structure (Web)

- Hero: full-bleed image with confident overlay type
- Section 1: Identity / proof
- Section 2: Service routing (Senior / Family)
- Section 3: Pull-quote or editorial statement (black background)
- Section 4: Portfolio grid (no captions)
- Section 5: Process or About teaser
- Section 6: Investment teaser
- Section 7: Inquiry CTA
- Footer: Locations served, NAP, navigation, social

### Mobile Considerations

- Same brand voice, denser visual rhythm
- Hero image scales to 9:16 or 4:5
- Type breaks at appropriate widths — never zoom-required
- 44×44px minimum tap targets
- Sticky inquiry CTA at the bottom of senior + family pages

## 11. Launch Checklist

Before any branded surface ships:

- [ ] Logo is exported at minimum 2x retina, with adequate clear space
- [ ] Color values are spec'd from this guide, not eyeballed
- [ ] Type pairing is Italiana/Cormorant + Inter — no third typeface
- [ ] Photography is consistent in editing voice
- [ ] Copy uses the approved word list and avoids the off-brand list
- [ ] Tone is appropriate to the channel
- [ ] Whitespace is generous
- [ ] Boys are visibly represented in any portfolio surface
- [ ] CTAs are inquiry-anchored, not "book now"
- [ ] NAP (name, address, phone) is verified and current

## 12. Evolution

This guide is meant to be revised at 12 and 24 months as EKP grows into new service lines:

- 12 months: incorporate finalized Personal Branding service line voice
- 24 months: introduce SML lifestyle family vertical sub-system if the vertical scales
- Ongoing: replace placeholder photography with the latest editorial work

The principles do not change. The execution gets sharper.
