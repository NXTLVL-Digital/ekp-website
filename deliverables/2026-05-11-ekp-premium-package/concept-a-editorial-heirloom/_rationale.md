# Concept A — Editorial Heirloom

A rationale doc for the first of three website concepts. This is the version closest to EKP's existing brand DNA — *elevated* significantly, not reinvented.

## Strategic Frame

Concept A is built for **Rachel (the Memory-Keeper Mom)** as the primary visitor, with Ella (the Style-Aware Senior) endorsing the choice. It assumes the visitor is at least a little intimidated by the price point and needs the site to *earn* the premium positioning in the first five seconds.

The design references are: Vogue masthead pages, T Magazine feature spreads, classic Vanity Fair portraits, and the website of Jose Villa Fine Art. It is intentionally classical — Concept B and Concept C take more design risk; Concept A is the safest premium move and the one most likely to convert without surprise.

## Voice / Headline

> *Portraits made for the wall, not the phone.*

This is the closing argument made the first line. Rachel reads it and immediately understands what makes EKP different from the cheaper alternatives.

## Design System

| Element | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Background | Cream (`#FAF7F2`) | Photo-friendly, warmer than pure white, on-brand |
| Display type | Italiana (fallback Cormorant Garamond) | Tall editorial serif matches the Yo Andy reference from the brand board |
| Body type | Inter | Clean geometric sans, Acrom substitute |
| Accent | Warm gold rules + hairline borders | Single premium signal |
| Hero | Full-bleed image with magazine-style "Issue / Volume" headline lockup | Magazine-cover allusion |
| Section numbering | Large gold serif "01 / 02 / 03" markers | Editorial TOC convention |
| Black bands | Used for pull quotes, dividers, and CTA sections | Signature EKP black-and-cream rhythm |

## Pages

1. **Home** — issue-cover hero, numbered service routing (Senior / Family), pull quote band, portfolio grid, journal teaser, booking CTA.
2. **About / Meet Emily** — two-column editorial intro, 5-step process timeline, pull quote band.
3. **Senior Portraits** — service-line hero, portfolio grid, dedicated senior-boys section, FAQ accordion, investment teaser.
4. **Family Portraits** — service hero, portfolio grid, dedicated Smith Mountain Lake section, investment teaser.
5. **Investment** — three-card pricing tier display (session / featured collection / family), heirloom product gallery.
6. **Portfolio** — categorized grids (Senior · Class of 2026/2027, Family · 2025–2026).
7. **Journal** — blog index, 6 sample posts with featured imagery.
8. **Contact** — inquiry form with phone, grad year, style direction; studio address + booking windows.

## Where This Concept Wins

- The visitor immediately understands the brand position
- Premium pricing feels earned, not imposed
- Parent buyer sees clear process, FAQ, and investment transparency
- Senior sees magazine aesthetic
- Boy-inclusive senior section addresses the largest market gap
- Smith Mountain Lake gets a dedicated touchpoint on the family page

## Where This Concept Takes Risk

- It looks more like *every* premium photography studio than Concept B does — the boldness comes from polish, not from rule-breaking
- Less Gen-Z-bait than Concept B (no oversized type acrobatics, no asymmetry)
- The audience that wants ultra-quiet luxury (Concept C buyer) may find it loud

## What Would Need to Be Truthful at Launch

- 18+ senior boy portraits in the senior portfolio (currently using placeholder Unsplash imagery)
- 12+ Smith Mountain Lake family portraits if launching that vertical
- Verified phone, email, studio address
- At least 6 real Journal posts to make the blog feel inhabited
